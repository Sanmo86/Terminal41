Paris-Charles de Gaulle (IATA: **CDG**) is France's main international gateway and the second-busiest airport in Europe, handling more than 70 million passengers a year. It sits about 25 km northeast of central Paris and serves as Air France's primary hub and the main SkyTeam gateway to Western Europe.

## Overview

CDG is spread across three terminal complexes connected by CDGVAL, a free automated shuttle train. It's one of the more sprawling major airports, so connections between terminals — especially between T1 and T3 — can take a while; always check your terminal and hall before heading out.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** A distinctive circular building mainly used by Star Alliance carriers.
- **Terminal 2 (Halls 2A–2G):** The largest cluster by far, home to Air France, SkyTeam partners, and most long-haul flights. Has its own RER B and TGV train stations. Always confirm which hall (2A through 2G) your flight uses — they're spread across a significant distance.
- **Terminal 3:** The smallest terminal, mainly used by low-cost carriers.

## Airlines

Air France dominates CDG as its home hub and the anchor of the SkyTeam alliance, alongside partners like Delta, KLM, and Korean Air. CDG also hosts a large number of long-haul international carriers, making it one of the best-connected airports in Europe for intercontinental routes.

## How to Get There

- **RER B Train:** **€14** one-way, with direct service from all terminals to central Paris stops like Châtelet–Les Halles, Saint-Michel, and Gare du Nord. About 35–50 minutes depending on stops and time of day.
- **Taxi (flat rate):** A regulated fixed fare applies regardless of traffic — **€56** to destinations on the Right Bank of the Seine, **€65** to the Left Bank, including luggage and up to four passengers. Booking via app or phone adds a small surcharge. About 45–60 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Bus (Lines 350/351):** **€2.50**, the cheapest option, but slow — about 60–90 minutes depending on traffic and route.
- **Ride-hailing:** Uber and other apps operate from designated pickup zones, at prices generally comparable to or slightly below the flat taxi fare.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check the operator's site for the current fare: [RATP](https://www.ratp.fr/en), [Paris Aéroport](https://www.parisaeroport.fr/en).*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Schengen flights:** Security only, no passport control. Typically **15–25 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–45 minutes** during morning and early-evening departure waves.
- **International (non-Schengen) flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **30–45 minutes** off-peak, but can stretch to **60–90 minutes** during peak international departure banks — CDG has a reputation for slower-than-average queues at Terminal 2E/2F during summer.
- Terminal 1's older layout and Terminal 2's sheer size both contribute to longer walking times between security and your gate — budget extra time beyond the security wait itself.

## Parking

All three terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks, plus more affordable off-site lots (PR and Orange Parks) connected by free shuttle. Pre-booking online is significantly cheaper than the walk-up rate.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available throughout CDG with no time cap — a traveler-friendly setup that makes it comfortable to work or stay connected during a layover.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Schengen flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is typically enough outside peak hours.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for Air France long-haul departures from Terminal 2E, where queues build up fast during the evening departure bank.
- Given CDG's size, add extra buffer time for walking between security and your gate, particularly in Terminal 2's more distant halls.

## Where to Eat

Terminal 2E and 2F have the strongest food selection, with French bistro-style restaurants alongside international chains. Terminal 1's dining options are more limited but include solid French classics. Expect premium pricing typical of a major European hub.

## Lounges

- **Air France lounges** (several across Terminal 2, including a flagship Business lounge) — accessible with eligible tickets, SkyTeam Elite Plus status, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available across the terminals.

## Duty-Free Shopping

CDG's duty-free areas, especially in Terminal 2E/2F, are known for an extensive selection of French luxury goods — perfume, wine, champagne, and fashion — making it one of the better airports in Europe for last-minute souvenir shopping.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours and a Schengen-eligible passport, central Paris is reachable via RER B in under an hour, making a short visit realistic — but factor in the return security queue.
- If staying airside, Terminal 2E/2F has the best mix of seating, food, and shopping for a long wait.
- CDG's size means overnight layovers require some planning — check with your airline or terminal for rest area options, as free sleeping areas are limited.
