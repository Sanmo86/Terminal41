Beijing Capital International Airport (IATA: **PEK**) is one of the busiest airports in the world and Air China's primary hub, handling more than 55 million passengers a year. It sits about 32 km (20 mi) northeast of central Beijing.

## Overview

PEK operates three terminals, with **Terminal 3** standing out as one of the largest airport terminal buildings ever built — when it opened for the 2008 Beijing Olympics, it was the largest single airport terminal in the world, designed by architect Norman Foster with a dragon-inspired roofline. Beijing now also has a second major airport, Daxing (PKX), but PEK remains a massive hub, particularly for Air China and Star Alliance — official operating data put its 2024 passenger throughput at [67.37 million, up 27.4% year-on-year](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/BEIJING-CAPITAL-INTERNATI-6170607/news/Beijing-Capital-International-Airport-December-2024-operation-data-48726895/) as international travel continued recovering.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** The airport's original, smaller terminal, handling a limited number of domestic flights.
- **Terminal 2:** A mix of domestic and some international carriers.
- **Terminal 3:** Air China's main hub terminal and the primary international gateway, so large that an internal shuttle train connects its far ends.

## Airlines

Air China, China's flag carrier, operates its primary hub at PEK, anchoring Terminal 3 with an extensive domestic network across the country plus international long-haul routes as a Star Alliance member. That hub concentration makes PEK one of the best-connected airports for travel to and within China, alongside Beijing's newer Daxing airport.

## How to Get There

- **Beijing Airport Express:** **¥25** flat fare, a dedicated subway line connecting directly to the city center (Sanyuanqiao/Dongzhimen) in about 20–30 minutes — the fastest and most predictable option.
- **Airport shuttle bus:** **¥16–30** depending on the route, with multiple lines covering different parts of the city, about 45–60 minutes.
- **Taxi:** **¥100–150** to central Beijing, about 40–60 minutes depending on traffic — comfortable but noticeably pricier than the train.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with the [Beijing Airport Express](http://www.bcia.com.cn/) or the airport's official transport page.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–50 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **30–45 minutes** off-peak, up to **55–80 minutes** during peak international departure banks — security at major Chinese airports tends to be thorough, so budget generous time.
- China's 144-hour visa-free transit policy applies at PEK for eligible nationalities — if using it, have your onward ticket and required paperwork ready, as the process is separate from standard immigration.

## Parking

All three terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks. Given the airport's scale and distance from the city, most visitors rely on the Airport Express, bus, or taxi rather than driving.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available throughout the airport, though registering with your passport number is typically required to connect — a standard requirement at Chinese airports.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is typically enough.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead — security and passport control processing can take longer than at other major hubs, especially during peak periods.
- Given Terminal 3's sheer size, budget real time to reach your gate after security, especially if you're departing from one of the terminal's far ends — the internal shuttle train ride itself takes several minutes.

## Where to Eat

PEK has an excellent selection of Beijing roast duck and regional Chinese cuisine alongside international chains, spread across all three terminals — Terminal 3 in particular has a wide range given its size and status as the main international gateway.

## Lounges

- **Air China lounges** are extensive across Terminal 3, reflecting its role as the airline's main hub, accessible with eligible tickets, elite status, or membership.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

PEK's duty-free zone includes Chinese specialties — tea, silk products, and Chinese liquor (baijiu) — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands, concentrated in Terminal 3's international departures area.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours and eligibility for the 144-hour visa-free transit policy (verify your nationality qualifies and bring your onward ticket), central Beijing is reachable via the Airport Express in about 20–30 minutes.
- If staying airside, Terminal 3 has by far the best mix of lounges, dining, and shopping for a long international wait — its sheer scale means there's genuinely a lot to explore without leaving security.
- Given Terminal 3's size, if your connection is tight, don't underestimate the walk (or shuttle ride) between distant gates — build in real buffer time.
