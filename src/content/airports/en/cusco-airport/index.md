Alejandro Velasco Astete International Airport (IATA: **CUZ**) is the main airport serving Cusco and the Sacred Valley, with more than 4 million passengers a year. It sits about 4 km from central Cusco — genuinely close by major-airport standards.

## Overview

CUZ operates a single terminal and functions as the real first step for most travelers heading to Machu Picchu — nearly everyone visiting the Inca citadel passes through here first, connecting onward by train and bus into the Sacred Valley, rather than flying directly from Lima. Sitting at roughly 3,400 meters (11,150 feet) above sea level, CUZ is also one of the higher-altitude major airports in the Americas.

## Terminals

CUZ operates around a single terminal, handling both regional flights (mainly to and from Lima) and a growing number of direct international routes.

## Airlines

LATAM Airlines operates an extensive network of flights connecting CUZ to Lima and other Peruvian cities, alongside a growing number of direct international routes given Cusco's importance as a global tourism destination. That connectivity makes CUZ one of the busiest tourism-driven airports in South America relative to its size.

## Getting There

- **Taxi:** **15–25 PEN** to central Cusco via the fixed-rate counter inside arrivals, about 10–15 minutes — genuinely one of the shortest airport transfers of any major South American tourism gateway.
- **Uber:** Generally comparable in price to a taxi, about 10–15 minutes.

*Prices above are approximate and subject to change — always check current fares with the taxi counter or Uber.*

## Security and Passport Control Wait Times

- **Regional flights:** Security plus immigration for most South American routes. Typically **15–20 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **25–35 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **20–30 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **35–50 minutes** during peak international departure banks.
- CUZ sees heavy seasonal variation tied to Machu Picchu tourism — expect longer waits during the June–August high season.

## Parking

The terminal has adjacent short- and long-stay parking, with clear signage.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available in the terminal with a time-limited free session.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Regional flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** before departure, especially during peak international departure periods.
- Given CUZ's high altitude, allow yourself time to acclimatize before jumping straight into strenuous activity — many travelers spend at least a day in Cusco before heading further into the Sacred Valley.

## Where to Eat

CUZ has a good selection of Andean Peruvian cuisine alongside limited international options, spread across the terminal.

## Lounges

- The **LATAM VIP Lounge** is available for eligible business class passengers, elite status holders, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

CUZ's duty-free area includes genuinely Peruvian specialties — alpaca textiles and coca tea (commonly used to help with altitude adjustment) — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Layover Tips

- With 4 hours or more and a visa situation that allows entry, central Cusco is reachable by taxi in about 10–15 minutes — genuinely close given CUZ's compact scale.
- If staying airside, note that CUZ isn't really designed as a long-layover destination — most travelers move quickly on to Cusco itself or straight toward the Sacred Valley.
- Given the altitude, plan at least a day or two of acclimatization in Cusco before attempting the Inca Trail or a full day at Machu Picchu — jumping straight into strenuous activity from sea level is a common and avoidable mistake.
