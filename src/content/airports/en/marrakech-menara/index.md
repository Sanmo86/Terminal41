Marrakech Menara Airport (IATA: **RAK**) is Morocco's second-busiest airport and the main gateway to Marrakech's Medina and the wider Atlas region. Remarkably close to the city — just 6 km from the Jemaa el-Fnaa square — it has seen fast-growing passenger numbers in step with Morocco's tourism boom, with strong charter and low-cost links to Europe.

## Overview

RAK operates two passenger terminals that share the same access roads and are a short walk apart. The airport is compact by international standards, which keeps landside connections simple, though passport control and customs can back up quickly during the peak winter and spring charter season when many European flights land in a short window.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** The main terminal, fully renovated with modern check-in, security, and boarding facilities, handling the bulk of both domestic and international traffic.
- **Terminal 2:** Complements Terminal 1, also handling domestic and international flights, with its own check-in, customs, and baggage reclaim areas.

Always confirm your terminal when booking a transfer or arranging pickup, since both handle a mix of domestic and international routes.

## Airlines

Royal Air Maroc is the dominant carrier and Morocco's flag airline, with an extensive domestic network plus growing international routes. Marrakech is also a major destination for European low-cost and charter carriers — Ryanair, easyJet, Transavia, and seasonal charter operators — reflecting the city's position as one of Morocco's top tourism draws.

## How to Get There

- **Airport Bus (Line 19):** **30 MAD** (~$3) one-way — the cheapest option, running regularly between the airport and the city center, about 25–45 minutes depending on traffic and stops.
- **Official Airport Taxi:** **100–150 MAD** by day, **150–240 MAD** at night for central destinations — a prepaid, fixed-rate system introduced in 2023 with no bargaining: pay at the taxi ordering counter, then head to your cab. About 20–30 minutes to the Medina.
- **Ride-hailing (Uber and similar):** Roughly **120–220 MAD**, depending on demand and time of day, similar journey time to a taxi.
- **Private transfer:** A pre-booked private transfer runs **150–300 MAD** depending on vehicle type — a good option if you're arriving late at night with luggage.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current rates at the airport taxi counter or your transfer operator.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **15–25 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–45 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **25–40 minutes** off-peak, but can stretch to **60–90 minutes** during peak charter season (winter and spring evenings), when several European flights land or depart in a short window.
- Immigration queues on arrival can be the slowest part of the process during high season — budget accordingly if you're being picked up on a schedule.

## Parking

Both terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks. As Marrakech is a compact airport, parking is straightforward, though it's worth confirming current rates directly with ONDA (the Moroccan airports authority) since options and pricing have been evolving alongside the airport's growth.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available in both terminals with a time-limited free session, typical for the region. An eSIM with a Moroccan or regional data plan is a reliable backup, especially useful for using ride-hailing apps or maps on arrival.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is generally sufficient outside peak periods.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially during the winter/spring high season when charter and low-cost banks can overwhelm check-in and security simultaneously.
- If you're being met by a riad or hotel driver, confirm your terminal and expected arrival-hall exit point in advance — the two terminals have separate arrivals areas.

## Where to Eat

Options are more limited than at major European or Asian hubs, but both terminals have cafés serving Moroccan mint tea and pastries alongside quick-service tagine and sandwich counters, plus a handful of international fast-food chains post-security.

## Lounges

Lounge options at RAK are more limited than at larger hub airports — mainly **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges with restricted capacity. If lounge access matters to you, check current availability before you fly, as options can change.

## Duty-Free Shopping

The duty-free area leans into Moroccan specialties — argan oil, spices, ceramics, and leather goods — alongside the standard fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands, making it a genuinely good last stop for gifts if you didn't have time to shop in the souks.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 4+ hours and a visa situation that allows entry, the Medina and Jemaa el-Fnaa are genuinely reachable — RAK is one of the closest major airports to a historic city center anywhere on this list, just 6 km away.
- If staying airside, seating and food options are more limited than at larger hubs, so plan accordingly for longer waits.
- Since lounge access is limited, arriving well-rested and hydrated matters more here than at airports with abundant premium options.
