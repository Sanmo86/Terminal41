Mexico City International Airport (IATA: **MEX**) is Mexico's busiest airport and Aeroméxico's principal hub, handling more than 50 million passengers a year. It sits just 5 km (3 mi) from the Zócalo, the heart of Mexico City's historic downtown — remarkably close for an airport of this size.

## Overview

MEX operates two terminals connected by a free shuttle (Aerotrén) since they aren't linked airside. As Aeroméxico's main hub, it anchors an extensive domestic network across Mexico plus international routes throughout North America, Europe, and Asia as a SkyTeam member. The airport's central location — genuinely close to downtown rather than out in the suburbs — is one of its defining practical advantages for travelers.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** A mix of domestic flights and some international carriers.
- **Terminal 2:** Aeroméxico's main terminal and its SkyTeam partners, handling the bulk of international departures.

The two terminals aren't connected within security — allow time for the Aerotrén shuttle if you need to transfer between them.

## Airlines

Aeroméxico, Mexico's flag carrier, operates its principal hub at MEX with an extensive domestic network across the country plus international routes to the US, Europe, and Asia as a member of SkyTeam. That hub concentration makes MEX the best-connected airport for travel within Mexico and one of the key gateways between North America and Latin America.

## How to Get There

- **Metro (Line 5):** **$5 MXN**, the cheapest option by far, about 30–40 minutes to the city center — best used in daylight with light luggage, given standard big-city metro safety considerations.
- **Authorized airport taxi (sitio):** **$250–350 MXN** via the official taxi counter inside the terminal — use this counter specifically rather than accepting offers from anyone outside, about 30–45 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Uber:** Generally comparable or slightly cheaper than the authorized taxi, about 30–45 minutes, with pickup from a designated area.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares at the airport's official taxi counter or your ride-hailing app.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–50 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **30–45 minutes** off-peak, up to **50–70 minutes** during peak international departure banks.
- MEX handles significant connecting traffic for both domestic and international Aeroméxico routes, so build extra time around peak morning and evening waves.

## Parking

MEX has parking available at both terminals, with clear signage for short- and long-stay options.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available in the terminals with a time-limited free session.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for Aeroméxico's peak international departure banks.
- Mexico City traffic can be unpredictable at rush hour — even though the airport is close to downtown in distance, budget extra time for the drive during peak commute hours.

## Where to Eat

MEX has a strong selection of Mexican regional cuisine — real tacos and regional specialties, not just generic airport fare — alongside international chains, spread across both terminals.

## Lounges

- The **Aeroméxico Salón Premier** lounges are available across Terminal 2 for eligible business class passengers, elite status holders, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

MEX's duty-free zone includes genuine Mexican specialties — tequila, silver jewelry, and local crafts — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 4+ hours and a visa situation permitting entry, downtown Mexico City and the Zócalo are genuinely reachable given the airport's unusually short distance from the center.
- If staying airside, Terminal 2 has the best mix of lounges and dining given Aeroméxico's hub presence.
- Since the two terminals aren't connected airside, confirm your terminal well before your connection and budget real time for the Aerotrén shuttle if you need to switch.
