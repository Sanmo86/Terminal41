Shanghai Pudong International Airport (IATA: **PVG**) is Asia's busiest airport by passenger traffic, serving more than 85 million travelers a year. It's China Eastern Airlines' primary hub and one of the country's two main international gateways, sitting about 30 km east of central Shanghai — far enough that fast transport options genuinely matter.

## Overview

PVG operates two terminals connected by an internal shuttle. Its most famous feature is the **Shanghai Maglev**, the world's fastest commercial train, which connects the airport to Longyang Road station on the edge of the city in about 8 minutes at speeds up to 431 km/h — from there, passengers transfer to the regular metro system for the rest of the journey into central Shanghai.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** Primarily SkyTeam and other international carriers.
- **Terminal 2:** China Eastern Airlines' main terminal, along with Star Alliance partners.

## Airlines

China Eastern Airlines dominates PVG as its home hub, operating an extensive domestic network across China alongside international routes as a SkyTeam member. Shanghai Airlines also has a significant presence. PVG serves as one of China's two primary gateways for international long-haul traffic, alongside Beijing.

## How to Get There

- **Maglev + Metro:** **¥50** for the maglev (¥40 with a same-day boarding pass), covering the 8-minute trip to Longyang Road at up to 431 km/h, then transfer to Metro Lines 2, 7, or 16 for the rest of the journey (an additional standard metro fare).
- **Metro Line 2:** **¥8**, the cheapest option, running directly into the city center but taking considerably longer — about 75–90 minutes.
- **Taxi:** **¥160–¥250** to central destinations like the Bund or People's Square, about 45–70 minutes depending on traffic — the most convenient but priciest option.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with [Shanghai Metro](https://www.shmetro.com/) or the [Shanghai Maglev](https://www.smtdc.com/) official site.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–50 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **30–45 minutes** off-peak, but can stretch to **60–90 minutes** during peak departure banks — Chinese airport security is generally thorough, so budget accordingly.
- Foreign visitors should have their passport and any required visa or transit documentation readily accessible, as checks can be more detailed than at some other major hubs.

## Parking

Both terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks. Given the airport's scale and distance from the city, most visitors rely on the maglev, metro, or taxi rather than driving.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available throughout the airport, though registration with your passport number is typically required to get online — a common requirement across Chinese airports.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is typically enough.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead — security and passport control processing can take longer than at some other major hubs, especially during peak periods.
- Given PVG's distance from the city (about 30 km), build in extra time for the journey to the airport itself, particularly if relying on the metro rather than the maglev.

## Where to Eat

PVG has a solid selection of regional Chinese cuisine alongside international chains, spread across both terminals. Options range from quick noodle counters to full sit-down restaurants.

## Lounges

- **China Eastern lounges** across Terminal 2, accessible with eligible tickets, elite status, or membership.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

PVG's duty-free area features Chinese specialties — tea, silk products, and Chinese liquor (baijiu) — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours and a visa situation that allows entry (China offers visa-free transit for many nationalities under certain conditions — check current rules before you fly), the Maglev makes central Shanghai genuinely reachable for a short visit.
- If staying airside, Terminal 2 has the better mix of lounges and dining for a long wait.
- Given PVG's distance from the city, the Maglev ride itself is worth experiencing even on a shorter layover if you have landside access — it's a unique experience in its own right.
