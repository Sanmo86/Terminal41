Dallas Fort Worth International Airport (IATA: **DFW**) is one of the busiest airports in the world and American Airlines' largest hub, serving more than 85 million passengers a year. It sits about 27 km northwest of downtown Dallas, roughly between Dallas and Fort Worth.

## Overview

DFW is famous for its sheer physical scale — at nearly 7,000 acres, it's larger in land area than the island of Manhattan. Five terminals (A through E) are arranged around a central oval and connected by the free automated **SkyLink train**, which is essential for moving between terminals; walking between distant ones isn't realistic.

## Terminals

- **Terminals A, B, C, D:** All handle a mix of American Airlines domestic and international flights, with Terminal D also serving as the primary international arrivals point.
- **Terminal E:** A mix of domestic carriers.

Always check your terminal, and budget real time for the SkyLink train if you're connecting between distant terminals — it's a genuine transit, not a quick walk.

## Airlines

American Airlines dominates DFW as its largest global hub, operating an enormous domestic network alongside major international routes to Europe, Latin America, and Asia. DFW's scale as American's primary connecting hub makes it one of the most-connected airports in the Americas.

## How to Get There

- **DART Orange Line:** **$2.50**, the cheapest option, taking about 50–60 minutes to reach downtown Dallas — the longest travel time of the three options but by far the most economical.
- **Taxi (flat rate):** A fixed fare of **$57** to downtown Dallas regardless of traffic, about 25–45 minutes depending on the route and time of day.
- **Uber / Lyft:** Typically **$25–$45** depending on time and demand, about 25–45 minutes — often the best balance of cost and speed.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with [DART](https://www.dart.org/) or your ride-hailing app.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

Like other US airports, DFW has no passport control for departing passengers — only TSA security screening, for both domestic and international flights alike:

- **Domestic departures (TSA security only):** Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–50 minutes** during busy morning and evening banks.
- **International departures (TSA security only):** Similar TSA process to domestic, typically **25–35 minutes** off-peak, up to **45–65 minutes** at peak.
- **Arriving internationally?** Customs and Border Protection (CBP) processing happens on arrival at Terminal D, separate from departure security — budget extra time if connecting onward from an international arrival.

## Parking

DFW has extensive parking directly at each terminal plus more affordable remote lots connected by shuttle. Given the airport's scale, pre-booking online and allowing extra time for shuttle service is worthwhile, especially during peak travel periods.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available throughout DFW with no time cap.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure; DFW's high volume of connecting American Airlines traffic means security can be unpredictable during peak banks.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, particularly for evening departures from Terminal D.
- Given DFW's sheer size, factor in real transit time via SkyLink if you're connecting between distant terminals — it's a genuine part of your journey, not a formality.

## Where to Eat

DFW has a strong selection of Texan food — proper Tex-Mex and barbecue alongside national chains — spread across all five terminals. As one of American's largest hubs, expect crowds at peak connection times.

## Lounges

- **American Admirals Club** locations are extensive across DFW, reflecting American's dominant presence — available with eligible tickets, elite status, or membership.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

Duty-free selections are fairly standard for a major US hub — fashion, cosmetics, and electronics — concentrated mainly around Terminal D's international departures area.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours, downtown Dallas is reachable via DART in about 50–60 minutes — the Bishop Arts District and downtown attractions are worth a look with a longer layover.
- If staying airside, all terminals have reasonable seating and food, though options and crowding vary by which American Airlines banks are currently connecting.
- Given DFW's scale, build extra buffer time into any tight connection — the SkyLink ride alone between distant terminals can take 10-15 minutes.
