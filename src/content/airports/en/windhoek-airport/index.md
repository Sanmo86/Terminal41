Hosea Kutako International Airport (IATA: **WDH**) is Namibia's main gateway and the base of FlyNamibia, with more than 1 million passengers a year. It sits about 45 km east of central Windhoek, the capital.

## Overview

WDH operates a single terminal and serves as the primary entry point to a country with a genuinely remarkable natural claim: the Namib Desert, widely considered the oldest desert on Earth at an estimated 55 to 80 million years old. The airport is the main gateway to Sossusvlei's towering red dunes, among the tallest sand dunes anywhere in the world, making WDH the starting point for one of Africa's most photographed landscapes.

## Terminals

WDH operates a single integrated terminal handling both regional and international traffic, including the full FlyNamibia network alongside Airlink's regional connections.

## Airlines

FlyNamibia, the country's main carrier, operates its base at WDH, connecting Windhoek to regional destinations within Namibia and neighboring countries. Airlink, a major South African regional carrier, adds further connections across Southern Africa, reflecting Namibia's position as a key link in the region's regional aviation network.

## Getting There

- **Airport shuttle:** **~350 NAD**, a direct service to central Windhoek timed to flight arrivals, about 40–50 minutes.
- **Taxi:** Typically **500–700 NAD** given the distance — notably expensive, about 40–50 minutes.
- **Rental car:** A very common choice, given that most visitors go on to self-drive across Namibia's vast distances to reach the desert and other destinations.

*Prices above are approximate and subject to change — always check current fares with the airport shuttle desk or your car rental company.*

## Security and Passport Control Wait Times

- **Regional flights:** Security only for most nearby Southern African routes. Typically **15–20 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **25–35 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **20–30 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **35–50 minutes** during peak international departure banks.
- WDH's relatively modest passenger volume generally keeps processing efficient outside of peak periods.

## Parking

The terminal has adjacent short- and long-stay parking, with clear signage — many visitors pick up rental cars directly at the airport.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available in the terminal with a limited free session.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Regional flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** before departure, especially during peak tourist season.
- Given WDH's distance from Windhoek, factor in real transfer time — the trip itself takes 40 minutes or more even by direct shuttle.

## Where to Eat

WDH has a good selection of Namibian cuisine, which carries a distinctive German influence from the country's colonial history, alongside more standard airport dining options.

## Lounges

The **Bidvest/Airlink Lounge** is available for eligible business class passengers and elite status holders.

## Duty-Free Shopping

WDH's duty-free area includes genuinely Namibian specialties — local crafts and semi-precious stones like tourmaline — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Layover Tips

- With 6 hours or more and a visa situation that allows entry, central Windhoek is reachable via shuttle or taxi in about 40–50 minutes — though given the airport's distance from town, most visitors treat Namibia as a dedicated destination rather than a stopover.
- If staying airside, WDH's dining offers a genuine taste of Namibia's distinctive German-influenced cuisine, a legacy of the country's colonial history rarely found combined this way elsewhere in Africa.
- Given how remote Namibia's key attractions are from the airport itself, most travelers pick up a rental car on arrival rather than relying on onward transport — plan accordingly if you're only passing through.
