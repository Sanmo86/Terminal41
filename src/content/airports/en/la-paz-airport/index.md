El Alto International Airport (IATA: **LPB**) is Bolivia's main international gateway and the primary hub of Boliviana de Aviación (BoA), with more than 3 million passengers a year. It sits about 11 km from central La Paz, in the city of El Alto, which itself sits above La Paz.

## Overview

LPB operates a single terminal and holds a genuinely extreme distinction: at roughly 4,061 meters (13,325 feet) above sea level, it's one of the highest international airports anywhere on Earth. The altitude is significant enough that some airlines advise arriving passengers about potential effects, and the thin air requires notably longer runways for safe takeoffs and landings.

## Terminals

LPB operates around a single terminal, handling both regional and international traffic, including the full network of Boliviana de Aviación.

## Airlines

Boliviana de Aviación (BoA), Bolivia's state-owned flag carrier, operates its principal hub at LPB, with an extensive domestic network across Bolivia's dramatic and varied terrain alongside international routes to neighboring South American countries and beyond. That network makes LPB the essential gateway for travel throughout Bolivia.

## Getting There

- **Taxi:** **60–100 BOB** to central La Paz via the fixed-rate counter inside arrivals, about 20–25 minutes.
- **Teleférico + taxi:** A cheaper combined option using La Paz's famous cable-car transit system, which connects El Alto (where the airport sits) down to La Paz proper — a genuinely unique and scenic way to descend into the city.

*Prices above are approximate and subject to change — always check current fares with the taxi counter or [Mi Teleférico](https://www.miteleferico.bo/).*

## Security and Passport Control Wait Times

- **Regional flights:** Security plus immigration for most South American routes. Typically **15–20 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **25–35 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **20–30 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **35–50 minutes** during peak international departure banks.
- LPB generally processes passengers efficiently given its more moderate overall traffic volume.

## Parking

The terminal has adjacent short- and long-stay parking, with clear signage.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available in the terminal with a time-limited free session.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Regional flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** before departure, especially during peak international departure periods.
- Given the altitude, some travelers experience mild effects upon arrival — take it easy for the first day if you're not acclimatized, rather than rushing straight into a packed itinerary.

## Where to Eat

LPB has a good selection of Bolivian cuisine alongside limited international options, spread across the terminal.

## Lounges

Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are available for eligible passengers, elite status holders, or paid access.

## Duty-Free Shopping

LPB's duty-free area includes genuinely Bolivian specialties — alpaca wool goods and coca-based products — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Layover Tips

- With 4 hours or more and a visa situation that allows entry, central La Paz is reachable by taxi in about 20–25 minutes, or via the scenic Teleférico cable-car system.
- If staying airside, take it easy given the altitude — even a short walk through the terminal can feel more tiring than expected at over 4,000 meters.
- Given LPB's extreme elevation, if your itinerary continues to lower-altitude destinations in Bolivia, be aware that some travelers feel notably better once they descend — plan accordingly if altitude affects you.
