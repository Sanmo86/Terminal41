Rome Fiumicino Airport (IATA: **FCO**), officially Leonardo da Vinci–Fiumicino Airport, is Italy's busiest airport and main international gateway, handling more than 49 million passengers a year. It sits about 32 km southwest of central Rome and is the primary hub for ITA Airways, Italy's national carrier.

## Overview

FCO operates two main terminals — Terminal 1 and Terminal 3 — after a reorganization that consolidated operations from the airport's older terminal buildings. Terminal 3, the larger of the two, handles both Schengen and non-Schengen flights and hosts most international carriers, while Terminal 1 is primarily used by ITA Airways.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** Primarily ITA Airways flights.
- **Terminal 3:** The larger terminal, serving both Schengen and non-Schengen flights and hosting most international airlines — this is where most long-haul arrivals and departures happen.

Always confirm your terminal, especially if you're connecting between ITA Airways and an international partner airline.

## Airlines

ITA Airways dominates FCO as its home hub and Italy's flag carrier, operating an extensive domestic and European network alongside long-haul routes to North America and Asia. FCO also hosts a large number of international carriers across both Schengen and non-Schengen routes, making it one of the best-connected airports in southern Europe.

## How to Get There

- **Leonardo Express:** **€14** one-way, a direct non-stop train to Roma Termini in about 32 minutes — the fastest and most straightforward option, running every 15–30 minutes.
- **FL1 Regional Train:** About **€8**, cheaper than the Leonardo Express but with more stops, taking around 45 minutes and serving stations like Trastevere and Tiburtina rather than terminating at Termini.
- **Taxi (flat rate):** **€55** to anywhere within the Aurelian Walls (central Rome) — this is the official rate confirmed by the airport authority; be sure you're in an official airport taxi, since some operators (like those with a "Comune di Fiumicino" plate) charge a higher fixed rate of €65. About 45–60 minutes depending on traffic.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with [Trenitalia](https://www.trenitalia.com/en) or the [official taxi information](https://www.adr.it/).*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Schengen flights:** Security only, no passport control. Typically **15–25 minutes** off-peak, up to **30–40 minutes** during morning and early-evening departure waves.
- **International (non-Schengen) flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **30–45 minutes** off-peak, but can stretch to **60–90 minutes** during peak international departure banks, especially in summer.
- Terminal 3's dual role serving both Schengen and non-Schengen flights means checkpoint configuration can vary — follow the signage carefully, especially during connections.

## Parking

Both terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks, plus more affordable off-site lots connected by shuttle. Pre-booking online is significantly cheaper than the walk-up rate.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available across the terminals with a time-limited free session; an eSIM with an Italian or EU data plan is a reliable backup for longer connectivity needs.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Schengen flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is typically enough outside peak hours.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for ITA Airways' long-haul departures during the evening bank.
- Rome's airport can get notably busy during peak tourist season (roughly May–September), so build in extra buffer time during those months.

## Where to Eat

FCO has a strong food selection reflecting Italian food culture — proper espresso bars, fresh pasta and pizza counters, and sit-down trattoria-style restaurants — concentrated mainly in Terminal 3. Prices are higher than in the city but the quality is generally a cut above typical airport fare.

## Lounges

- **ITA Airways lounges** across Terminal 1 and Terminal 3, accessible with eligible tickets, elite status, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available across both terminals.

## Duty-Free Shopping

FCO's duty-free areas are strong on Italian specialties — wine, olive oil, and gourmet food — alongside high-end Italian fashion brands and the usual international cosmetics and electronics, particularly extensive in Terminal 3.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours and a Schengen-eligible passport, central Rome is reachable via the Leonardo Express in about 32 minutes — the Colosseum and historic center are a short walk or metro ride from Termini, making a short visit realistic.
- If staying airside, Terminal 3 has the best mix of lounges, dining, and shopping for a long wait — and check for any current art or archaeology exhibitions in the terminal.
- Given FCO's distance from the city (about 32 km), factor in real transfer time on both ends of a city visit — don't cut it too close on the return trip through security.
