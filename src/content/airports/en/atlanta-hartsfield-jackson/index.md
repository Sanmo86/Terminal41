Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (IATA: **ATL**) is, by total passenger volume, the busiest airport in the world, serving more than 108 million travelers a year. It's Delta Air Lines' largest hub and a single, sprawling airport built around a linear string of concourses rather than the multi-terminal-building layout common at other major hubs.

## Overview

ATL's design is genuinely different from most large airports: instead of separate terminal buildings, it's organized as a Domestic Terminal (with concourses T, A, B, and C) and an International Terminal, Concourse F, connected to concourses D and E. All seven concourses are linked by the underground **Plane Train**, an automated people mover that's essential for getting around — walking between distant concourses is possible via connector corridors but can take a long time.

## Terminals

- **Domestic Terminal (Concourses T, A, B, C):** The bulk of Delta's massive domestic network.
- **Concourses D and E:** A mix of domestic and some international gates.
- **Concourse F (Maynard H. Jackson International Terminal):** Dedicated international arrivals and departures, including its own security checkpoint and passport control area.

Given the airport's linear layout, always check your concourse — a same-airport connection between, say, Concourse T and Concourse F is a genuine cross-airport trip via the Plane Train, not a short walk.

## Airlines

Delta Air Lines dominates ATL as its largest global hub, operating an enormous domestic network alongside a growing number of international routes. Southwest and Frontier also have a significant presence for domestic travel, making ATL one of the most connected airports in North America for onward flights anywhere in the US.

## How to Get There

- **MARTA Train:** **$2.50**, the cheapest option, departing directly from Airport Station under the Domestic Terminal to Five Points (downtown) in about 20 minutes, with connections across the wider MARTA rail network.
- **Taxi (flat rate):** A regulated flat fare of roughly **$30–$35** applies to the downtown zone (plus a small fuel surcharge and extra-passenger fees), about 20–30 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Uber / Lyft:** Typically **$28–$36** for UberX to downtown, with Lyft running slightly lower — prices vary with time of day and demand, about 20–30 minutes.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with [MARTA](https://www.itsmarta.com/) or your ride-hailing app.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

Like other US airports, ATL has no passport control for departing passengers — only TSA security screening, for both domestic and international flights alike:

- **Domestic departures (TSA security only):** Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–50 minutes** during the busiest morning and evening domestic banks — ATL handles an enormous volume of connecting traffic, so security lines can build quickly at peak times.
- **International departures (TSA security only):** Similar TSA process to domestic, typically **25–35 minutes** off-peak, up to **45–60 minutes** at peak, concentrated around Concourse F.
- **Arriving internationally?** Customs and Border Protection (CBP) processing happens on arrival at Concourse F, separate from the departure security process — budget extra time if you're connecting onward from an international arrival, as it involves collecting and rechecking bags before continuing.

## Parking

ATL has extensive parking directly at the terminal plus more affordable economy lots connected by shuttle. Given the sheer scale of the airport, pre-booking online and checking current wait times for shuttle service is worthwhile, especially during peak travel periods.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available throughout ATL with no time cap.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure; ATL's high volume of connecting traffic means security lines can be unpredictable, so err on the earlier side during peak morning and evening banks.
- **International flights:** Arrive **2.5–3 hours** ahead, particularly for evening departures from Concourse F.
- If you're arriving internationally with a tight connection, budget significant extra time for CBP processing and rechecking bags before your next flight — this is one of the more common sources of missed connections at ATL.

## Where to Eat

ATL has a strong selection of Southern food, including a heavy Chick-fil-A presence (Atlanta being its hometown), alongside national chains and sit-down options spread across all concourses. As one of the world's busiest airports, expect crowds at peak dining times.

## Lounges

- **Delta Sky Club** locations are extensive across ATL, reflecting Delta's dominant presence — available with eligible tickets, elite status, or membership.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

Duty-free selections are fairly standard for a major US hub — fashion, cosmetics, electronics, and Southern-themed souvenirs — concentrated mainly around Concourse F's international departures area.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours, downtown Atlanta is reachable via MARTA in about 20 minutes — attractions like the Georgia Aquarium and World of Coca-Cola are a short walk from the Peachtree Center MARTA station.
- If staying airside, all concourses have reasonable seating and food options, though Concourse F tends to be less crowded outside peak international departure times.
- Given ATL's sheer size and connecting-traffic volume, build extra buffer time into any tight connection — the Plane Train ride between distant concourses alone can take 10-15 minutes.
