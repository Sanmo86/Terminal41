Miami International Airport (IATA: **MIA**) is American Airlines' largest international gateway and the principal US hub for travel to Latin America and the Caribbean, handling more than 52 million passengers a year. It sits about 13 km (8 mi) west of downtown Miami — notably close for a major US hub.

## Overview

MIA operates as a single large terminal complex divided into North, Central, and South sections, each with its own set of concourses. Its defining role is as the "Gateway of the Americas" — more flights depart MIA for Latin America and the Caribbean than from any other US airport, making it a genuinely distinctive hub within the American aviation landscape, more international in character than most US airports of similar size.

## Terminals

- **North Terminal (Concourse D):** American Airlines' primary concourse, handling the bulk of its domestic and Latin American network.
- **Central Terminal (Concourses E, F, G):** A mix of international carriers and additional American Airlines gates.
- **South Terminal (Concourses H, J):** International long-haul carriers, including many Latin American and European airlines.

All sections are connected by the free **MIA Mover** automated train, which also links to the rental car center.

## Airlines

American Airlines operates its largest international hub at MIA, anchoring the North Terminal with an extensive network across Latin America, the Caribbean, and beyond, alongside significant domestic and transatlantic routes. That concentration makes MIA one of the best-connected airports in the world for travel between the US and the region south of it.

## How to Get There

- **Miami Metrorail (Orange Line):** **$2.25** one-way, the cheapest option, about 30 minutes to downtown Miami with a free connection via the MIA Mover to the Metrorail station.
- **Taxi:** Flat rates apply to most zones — typically **$35–50** to downtown or South Beach, about 20–30 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Uber / Lyft:** Generally **$25–40** depending on demand, about 20–30 minutes.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with [Miami-Dade Transit](https://www.miamidade.gov/global/transportation/home.page) or your ride-hailing app.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

Like other US airports, MIA has no departure passport control — only TSA security screening, for both domestic and international departures:

- **Domestic departures (TSA only):** Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–50 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International departures (TSA only):** Similar TSA process to domestic, typically **25–35 minutes** off-peak, up to **45–65 minutes** at peak — MIA's huge Latin America departure volume means certain evening banks get busy.
- **Arriving internationally?** Customs and Border Protection (CBP) processing happens on arrival, separate from departure security — MIA handles an enormous volume of international arrivals, so budget extra time if connecting onward.

## Parking

MIA has extensive parking directly at the terminal plus more affordable economy options connected by shuttle. Pre-booking online is generally cheaper than the walk-up rate.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available throughout the terminal with no time limit.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for the heavy evening departure banks to Latin America and the Caribbean.
- Given MIA's outsized role as a Latin America gateway, flights to that region can see particularly high check-in and security volume during peak holiday travel periods — build in extra buffer around major Latin American holidays.

## Where to Eat

MIA's food scene stands out among US airports for its genuine Latin American character — Cuban coffee windows (order a colada and watch locals do the same) and a strong lineup of Latin American cuisine alongside the usual national chains, spread across all three terminal sections.

## Lounges

- **American Admirals Club** lounges are extensive given American's hub presence, and a **Centurion Lounge** is also available, both accessible with eligible tickets, elite status, or membership.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are available as well.

## Duty-Free Shopping

MIA's duty-free selection is fairly standard for a major US gateway — fashion, cosmetics, and electronics — spread across the international departure areas of all three terminal sections.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 5+ hours, South Beach is reachable via Uber/Lyft in about 25–30 minutes — a quick taste of the beach and Art Deco district is realistic on a longer layover.
- If staying airside, grab a Cuban coffee at one of the airport's classic ventanitas (coffee windows) — a small but genuine taste of Miami culture without leaving the terminal.
- Given MIA's role as the main US-Latin America gateway, if you're connecting internationally, double-check whether your onward flight requires re-clearing security, since MIA's mix of domestic and international connections isn't always a simple walk between gates.
