London Heathrow (IATA: **LHR**) is Europe's busiest airport and one of the most important long-haul hubs in the world, connecting London to more countries than almost any other airport. It sits about 24 km west of central London and serves as British Airways' main base and a major Star Alliance and oneworld gateway.

## Overview

Heathrow operates four active terminals — Terminal 1 permanently closed in 2015. Unlike more compact hub airports, Heathrow's terminals are spread across a large site and aren't within walking distance of each other; moving between them, even airside, requires a dedicated shuttle train or bus. Always check your terminal well before travel, since it directly affects which train station or drop-off point you need.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 2:** Mainly Star Alliance carriers.
- **Terminal 3:** A mix of oneworld and other international carriers, including Virgin Atlantic.
- **Terminal 4:** Primarily SkyTeam carriers and others.
- **Terminal 5:** Exclusively British Airways (and Iberia) — one of the largest single-airline terminals in the world, well organized but large enough that connections within it still take real time.

## Airlines

British Airways dominates Heathrow as its home hub and the anchor of the oneworld alliance in Europe, based at Terminal 5. Virgin Atlantic, United, and Qatar Airways are also major operators, alongside dozens of international flag carriers — Heathrow has more long-haul international connections than almost any other airport in the world.

## How to Get There

- **Elizabeth Line:** **£15.50** flat fare regardless of time of day, direct to Paddington in about 30–35 minutes, continuing across central London without changing trains.
- **Heathrow Express:** **£25** for a walk-up single (as low as £10 if booked ~90 days ahead), the fastest option at just 15 minutes non-stop to Paddington.
- **Piccadilly Line (London Underground):** **~£5.90** from Zone 1, the cheapest rail option but the slowest — about 50–60 minutes with many stops.
- **Taxi:** A metered black cab typically runs **£75–£130** depending on traffic and destination; Uber and other ride-hailing apps tend to be somewhat cheaper, roughly **£55–£90**. About 45–75 minutes depending on traffic.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check the operator's site for the current fare: [Transport for London](https://tfl.gov.uk/), [Heathrow Express](https://www.heathrowexpress.com/).*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **UK/domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **40–50 minutes** during morning and early-evening waves.
- **International flights:** Security plus UK Border Force passport control (or eGates for eligible passport holders). Typically **35–50 minutes** off-peak, but can stretch to **60–90 minutes** during peak periods — Heathrow has had a reputation for longer-than-average queues at various points, though eGates have helped for many nationalities.
- Given the distances between terminals, if you're connecting between airlines in different terminals, budget significant extra time beyond the security wait itself for the inter-terminal transfer.

## Parking

All four terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks, plus more affordable off-site "Long Stay" options connected by free shuttle. Pre-booking online is significantly cheaper than the walk-up rate.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available throughout Heathrow with no time cap.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **UK/domestic flights:** **2 hours** before departure is generally recommended.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for Terminal 5's British Airways long-haul departures during the evening bank.
- If you're transferring between terminals, add 30–45 minutes beyond your security-wait estimate to account for the shuttle train or bus.

## Where to Eat

Terminal 5 has the widest selection, including proper British pub food, M&S Simply Food, and a range of global chains. The other terminals have solid but somewhat smaller selections. Prices are typically high, in line with major European hub airports.

## Lounges

- **British Airways lounges** across Terminal 5 (including a Concorde Room for top-tier passengers) — among the more extensive lounge networks of any single airline globally.
- Numerous **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are available across all four terminals.

## Duty-Free Shopping

Heathrow's duty-free areas are known for British brands — whisky, fashion, and gourmet food — alongside the usual international luxury names, particularly strong in Terminal 5's departures area.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours and a visa situation that allows UK entry, central London is reachable in as little as 15 minutes via the Heathrow Express — but factor in the return security queue, which can be substantial.
- If staying airside, Terminal 5 has by far the most comfortable seating, dining, and shopping for a long wait.
- Given Heathrow's terminal layout, if your layover involves a terminal change, treat it as a genuine leg of your journey — check the shuttle schedule rather than assuming a quick walk.
