Istanbul Airport (IATA: **IST**) is one of the largest airports in the world by terminal floor space and has become Europe's busiest international airport, handling more than 84 million passengers a year. It's the primary hub for Turkish Airlines, whose extensive route network makes IST one of the most connected airports on the planet, particularly for routes into Africa, Central Asia, and the Middle East.

## Overview

Unlike most airports of its size, IST is built as a single continuous mega-terminal under one roof, divided into five concourses rather than separate buildings. Opened in 2018 to replace the old Atatürk Airport, it was designed with huge growth capacity in mind — the scale is genuinely impressive, but it also means long walking distances; moving walkways and internal transit are essential for getting between distant gates.

## Terminals

IST operates as **one mega-terminal** with five concourses:
- **Concourses A, B, D, and F:** Primarily international flights.
- **Concourse G:** Primarily domestic flights.

Because it's a single building, there's no need to transfer between separate terminal structures — but the sheer size means budgeting real walking time between concourses, especially for connections.

## Airlines

Turkish Airlines dominates IST as its home hub, operating one of the most extensive international route networks of any airline, with particularly strong connectivity to Africa and Central Asia that's harder to find via other European hubs. Budget carrier Pegasus also has a significant presence, primarily for domestic and regional routes.

## How to Get There

- **M11 Metro:** Around **₺40** (with an İstanbulkart; a higher amount is deducted and refunded automatically), running directly from the airport to Gayrettepe on the European side in about 35 minutes, with onward connections into the wider metro network.
- **Havaist Bus:** **₺318–₺468** depending on destination, serving 12 points across the city including Taksim (~90 minutes) and the Otogar bus terminal (~45 minutes) — a good option if your destination isn't directly on the metro line.
- **Taxi:** Roughly **₺1,500–₺2,000** (about $32–$43) to the European-side city center including tolls, about 40–60 minutes depending on traffic — IST is notably far from central Istanbul, so factor in real travel time.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with [Havaist](https://www.hava.ist/) or the [İstanbulkart](https://www.istanbulkart.istanbul/) system.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **15–25 minutes** off-peak, up to **30–40 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **30–45 minutes** off-peak, but can stretch to **60–90 minutes** during peak departure banks — IST's scale means queues can look long but often move steadily; still, budget generously given the airport's size.
- Given the concourse layout, add extra time for walking between security and distant gates — this is one of the largest airport buildings in the world, and distances are genuinely significant.

## Parking

IST has extensive parking directly connected to the terminal. Given the airport's scale, clear signage helps, but pre-booking online for busy travel periods is worth considering.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available throughout IST with a time-limited free session; an eSIM with a Turkish data plan is a reliable backup for longer connectivity needs.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is generally sufficient.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for Turkish Airlines' peak evening departure banks, when the airport's scale and passenger volume combine to make things busiest.
- Given IST's size, add extra buffer time purely for walking between security and your gate — this is a meaningfully bigger airport than most passengers are used to.

## Where to Eat

IST has a strong selection of Turkish food — from quick simit and tea stands to full sit-down restaurants serving kebabs and Turkish breakfast spreads — alongside the usual international chains. Turkish tea houses scattered through the concourses are a genuinely pleasant way to pass time.

## Lounges

- **Turkish Airlines CIP Lounge**, one of the largest and most highly rated airline lounges in the world, spanning multiple floors with extensive dining and rest areas — available with eligible tickets or elite status.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

IST's duty-free area is extensive, with a strong focus on Turkish specialties — Turkish delight, tea, ceramics, and textiles — alongside the usual international fashion and cosmetics brands, spread across a large, well-stocked shopping zone.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours on a Turkish Airlines ticket, check eligibility for the free **Touristanbul** guided city tour program — it's one of the more genuinely useful long-layover perks offered by any airline.
- If staying airside, the Turkish Airlines CIP Lounge is worth prioritizing if you have access — it's large enough to comfortably spend hours in.
- Given IST's distance from the city center (about 35 km), a self-arranged trip into Istanbul needs a longer layover than at more centrally located airports — 8+ hours is a safer minimum if you're not using the airline's tour program.
