Frankfurt Airport (IATA: **FRA**) is Germany's busiest airport and one of Europe's most important hubs, serving more than 63 million passengers a year. It's the primary hub for Lufthansa and a major Star Alliance gateway, sitting just 12 km southwest of central Frankfurt.

## Overview

FRA operates two terminals connected by the free Skyline shuttle train. One of its most distinctive features is a dedicated long-distance rail station attached directly to Terminal 1, integrating the airport into Germany's national ICE high-speed rail network — a level of rail integration few other major airports match.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** The larger and older terminal, home to Lufthansa and most Star Alliance carriers, with direct access to both the regional S-Bahn station and the long-distance ICE train station.
- **Terminal 2:** Handles a range of other international carriers, connected to Terminal 1 by the free Skyline shuttle.

## Airlines

Lufthansa dominates FRA as its largest hub and the anchor of the Star Alliance, operating an extensive network across Europe and long-haul routes worldwide. Condor also has a notable presence for leisure routes. FRA's Star Alliance concentration makes it one of the best-connected airports in Europe.

## How to Get There

- **S-Bahn Train:** **€6.90** for a single RMV ticket, direct to Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof (main station) in about 11–15 minutes — the fastest and most straightforward option into the city center.
- **Taxi:** Metered, typically **€30–€40** to the city center, about 20–30 minutes depending on traffic.
- **ICE High-Speed Train:** Prices vary by destination, but this is a genuinely unique option — board directly from the airport's own long-distance station and travel to Cologne, Munich, Berlin, and other major German cities without going into Frankfurt at all.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with [Deutsche Bahn](https://www.bahn.com/en) or the [RMV regional transport authority](https://www.rmv.de/en).*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Schengen flights:** Security only, no passport control. Typically **15–25 minutes** off-peak, up to **30–40 minutes** during morning and early-evening departure waves.
- **International (non-Schengen) flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **30–45 minutes** off-peak, but can stretch to **60–90 minutes** during peak international departure banks — FRA's scale as one of Europe's largest hubs means queues can build quickly during Lufthansa's peak connecting waves.
- Given the volume of connecting passengers, FRA's security process is generally efficient but high-capacity — expect a well-organized but sometimes long queue rather than chaos.

## Parking

Both terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks, plus more affordable off-site lots connected by shuttle. Pre-booking online is cheaper than the walk-up rate.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available across the terminals with a time-limited free session; an eSIM with a German or EU data plan is a reliable backup for longer connectivity needs.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Schengen flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is typically enough outside peak hours.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for Lufthansa's long-haul departures during peak connecting banks.
- FRA's high volume of connecting traffic means security lines can be unpredictable at peak times — err on the earlier side during Lufthansa's major morning and evening connection waves.

## Where to Eat

FRA has a strong selection of German food — pretzels, sausages, and hearty regional dishes — alongside international chains and beer garden-style options, concentrated mainly in Terminal 1. Prices are on the higher side, typical of a major European hub.

## Lounges

- **Lufthansa lounges** across Terminal 1, including First Class Terminal facilities for top-tier passengers — one of the most extensive lounge networks of any single airline in Europe.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available across both terminals.

## Duty-Free Shopping

FRA's duty-free areas are known for German specialties — eyewear, kitchen brands, chocolate, and Riesling wine — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours and a Schengen-eligible passport, central Frankfurt is reachable via S-Bahn in about 15 minutes, making a short visit to the Römerberg old town realistic.
- If staying airside, Terminal 1 has by far the best mix of lounges, dining, and shopping for a long wait.
- If your itinerary has flexibility, remember FRA's direct ICE connections mean a long layover could even be used to visit a different German city entirely, not just Frankfurt.
