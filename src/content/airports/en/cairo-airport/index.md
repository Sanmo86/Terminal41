Cairo International Airport (IATA: **CAI**) is Egypt's largest and busiest airport, serving more than 31 million passengers a year as the country's primary international gateway. It sits about 15 km northeast of central Cairo and is the main hub for EgyptAir and the Star Alliance's gateway to Egypt.

## Overview

CAI operates three main terminals plus a seasonal terminal used during Hajj and Umrah pilgrimage periods. Terminal 3, the newest and most modern, handles EgyptAir and most Star Alliance flights, while Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 split domestic, regional, and other international traffic. Always confirm your terminal, since transfers between them require a shuttle bus rather than a walk.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** The oldest terminal, mostly domestic and regional flights.
- **Terminal 2:** Recently renovated, handling a mix of regional and international carriers.
- **Terminal 3:** The largest and newest, the main hub for EgyptAir and Star Alliance airlines.
- **Seasonal Flights Terminal:** Used during peak Hajj and Umrah pilgrimage periods.

## Airlines

EgyptAir dominates CAI as its home hub and Egypt's flag carrier, operating an extensive domestic network alongside international routes across Africa, Europe, the Middle East, and Asia as a Star Alliance member. Nile Air also has a growing presence for domestic and regional routes.

## How to Get There

- **BRT + Metro:** Under **20 EGP** total, the cheapest option — a dedicated BRT bus connects the airport to Adly Mansour metro station, with onward connections into central Cairo via Line 3, about 45–60 minutes depending on transfers.
- **Official Taxi:** Roughly **200–450 EGP** to Downtown Cairo — confirm the driver is using the meter, or agree on a price before departing, since some drivers skip the meter. About 30–60 minutes depending on Cairo's often heavy traffic.
- **Uber / Careem:** Typically **120–200 EGP**, often cheaper and more straightforward than a taxi since the fare is shown upfront in the app, about 30–60 minutes.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with your ride-hailing app or agree on a taxi price before you get in.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–50 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control, plus visa-on-arrival processing for many nationalities. Typically **30–45 minutes** off-peak, but can stretch to **60–90 minutes** during peak periods — arrival processing in particular can take longer if you need to purchase a visa-on-arrival stamp before reaching the passport counter.
- If you haven't arranged an e-Visa in advance, budget extra time for the visa-on-arrival counter, which typically requires exact cash payment (commonly $25 USD).

## Parking

All terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks. Given ongoing modernization at the airport, it's worth confirming current rates and availability directly with Cairo Airport Company.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available across the terminals with a time-limited free session; an eSIM with an Egyptian data plan is a reliable backup, especially useful for ride-hailing apps and maps on arrival.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is generally sufficient.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, particularly if you need to purchase a visa-on-arrival, which adds a step before passport control.
- Cairo's traffic can be unpredictable and heavy at various times of day, so build in extra buffer time for the journey to the airport itself, not just processing once you arrive.

## Where to Eat

Terminal 3 has the strongest dining selection, with Egyptian classics — koshari, falafel, fresh juices — alongside international fast-food chains and sit-down options. Terminals 1 and 2 have more limited but functional choices.

## Lounges

- **EgyptAir lounges** in Terminal 3, accessible with eligible tickets, elite status, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available, mainly in Terminal 3.

## Duty-Free Shopping

CAI's duty-free area features Egyptian specialties — perfumes and essential oils, spices, papyrus art, and jewelry — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands, concentrated in Terminal 3.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 5+ hours and a visa arranged (on arrival or in advance), a trip to see the Pyramids of Giza is technically possible but tight given Cairo's traffic and airport processing time — a 7+ hour layover is a safer minimum for a worthwhile visit.
- If staying airside, Terminal 3 has by far the best mix of lounges, dining, and duty-free for a long wait.
- Budget extra time for both the visa-on-arrival process and Cairo's traffic if you're leaving the airport, in either direction.
