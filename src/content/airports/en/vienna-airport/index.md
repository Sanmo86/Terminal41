Vienna International Airport (IATA: **VIE**) is Austria's busiest airport and the primary hub of Austrian Airlines, a member of the Star Alliance and Lufthansa Group, with more than 31 million passengers a year. It sits about 18 km southeast of central Vienna.

## Overview

VIE operates three terminals and has built a genuinely distinctive niche among Europe's major hubs: leveraging Vienna's historic role as a crossroads between Western and Eastern Europe, the airport offers more direct connections into Eastern Europe, the Balkans, and former Soviet states than any other airport in Western Europe. That legacy network, combined with consistently strong service ratings, has made VIE a reliable connecting point for travelers heading in that direction.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1 & 1A:** Handles a mix of Star Alliance partner airlines and other international carriers.
- **Terminal 3 (SkyLink):** The main hub terminal for Austrian Airlines and its Star Alliance partners, connected to Terminal 1A via an internal walkway.

## Airlines

Austrian Airlines operates its principal hub at VIE, running an extensive network across Europe alongside its distinctive strength in Eastern European and Balkan connections, as a member of the Star Alliance and part of the Lufthansa Group. That network depth makes VIE one of the most useful connecting airports in Europe for travel toward Eastern Europe, Russia's neighboring states, and the wider region.

## Getting There

- **City Airport Train (CAT):** **€14.90**, a non-stop express service to Wien Mitte in about 16 minutes — the fastest option.
- **S-Bahn (S7):** **€4.30**, the cheaper regional rail option, about 25 minutes with more stops.
- **Taxi:** Typically **€36–45** to the city center via the official taxi rank, about 20–25 minutes depending on traffic.

*Prices above are approximate and subject to change — always check current fares with [Wien Mitte / CAT](https://www.cityairporttrain.com/) or the [Wiener Linien](https://www.wienerlinien.at/) app.*

## Security and Passport Control Wait Times

- **Schengen flights:** Security only for most intra-EU/Schengen routes. Typically **15–20 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **25–35 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **25–35 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **40–60 minutes** during peak international departure banks.
- VIE generally processes passengers efficiently even during Austrian Airlines' busy connecting waves.

## Parking

Both terminal areas have adjacent short- and long-stay parking, with clear signage. Booking online ahead of arrival is cheaper than the walk-up rate.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available throughout the airport with no time limit.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Schengen flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** before departure, especially during Austrian Airlines' peak connecting banks.
- Given VIE's role as a major connecting hub, allow extra time if your itinerary involves a tight transfer between Terminal 1A and Terminal 3.

## Where to Eat

VIE has a solid selection of Austrian and Viennese cuisine alongside international chains, spread across its terminals.

## Lounges

- The **Austrian Airlines Business Lounge** is available in Terminal 3 for eligible business class passengers, elite status holders, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

VIE's duty-free area includes genuinely Austrian specialties — chocolate and Riedel glassware — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Layover Tips

- With 4 hours or more and a visa situation that allows entry, central Vienna and the Ringstrasse are reachable via CAT in about 16 minutes.
- If staying airside, Terminal 3 has the strongest combination of lounges and dining given the concentration of Austrian Airlines operations there.
- Given Vienna's distinctive strength in Eastern European connections, this is a genuinely useful hub to know if you're regularly connecting toward that part of the world — it's often a smoother routing than larger Western European hubs offer.
