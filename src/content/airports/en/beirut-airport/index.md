Beirut–Rafic Hariri International Airport (IATA: **BEY**) is Lebanon's main gateway and the hub of Middle East Airlines (MEA), the national flag carrier, with more than 7 million passengers a year. It sits about 9 km south of central Beirut, close to the coast.

## Overview

BEY operates a single terminal and serves a city with a genuinely remarkable claim to history: Beirut has a documented history stretching back more than 5,000 years, making it one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on Earth. Layers of Phoenician, Roman, Ottoman, and French colonial influence sit beneath the modern city, and the airport's coastal approach along the Mediterranean gives arriving passengers a striking first look at that layered history.

## Terminals

BEY operates a single terminal building organized into multiple halls, handling both regional and international traffic, including the full Middle East Airlines network.

## Airlines

Middle East Airlines (MEA), Lebanon's flag carrier, operates its hub at BEY, running an extensive network connecting Beirut to Europe, the Gulf, and the wider Middle East. As one of the region's longest-established carriers, MEA's presence anchors BEY as a key connecting point for travel across the Eastern Mediterranean.

## Getting There

- **Taxi:** **~15–25 USD**, agree on the fare before departing or use the official taxi desk inside the terminal, about 15–20 minutes.
- **Ride-hailing app:** Comparable in price to a taxi, widely used in Beirut, about 15–20 minutes.
- **Hotel shuttle:** Many hotels in Beirut offer pre-arranged airport pickup, often included with booking.

*Prices above are approximate and subject to change — always confirm fares with the official taxi desk or your ride-hailing app.*

## Security and Passport Control Wait Times

- **Regional flights:** Security only for most nearby Middle East routes. Typically **15–20 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **25–35 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **20–30 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **35–50 minutes** during peak international departure banks.
- BEY generally processes passengers efficiently outside of MEA's peak connecting periods.

## Parking

The terminal has adjacent short- and long-stay parking, with clear signage.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available throughout the airport with no time limit.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Regional flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** before departure, especially during MEA's peak departure periods.
- Given BEY's proximity to central Beirut, transfer time is genuinely less of a concern here than at many other major Middle Eastern hubs.

## Where to Eat

BEY has a strong selection of Lebanese cuisine, including proper mezze, alongside international chains, spread across the terminal.

## Lounges

- The **MEA Cedar Lounge** is available for eligible business class passengers, elite status holders, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

BEY's duty-free area includes genuinely Lebanese specialties — wine, arak, and baklava — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Layover Tips

- With 4 hours or more and a visa situation that allows entry, central Beirut is reachable via taxi in about 15–20 minutes — genuinely close given the airport's proximity to the city.
- If staying airside, BEY's dining offers a real taste of Lebanese mezze culture, worth exploring even on a shorter connection.
- Given Beirut's more than 5,000 years of continuous history, even a short city visit during a longer layover touches layers of Phoenician, Roman, and Ottoman heritage rarely found together in one place.
