Los Angeles International Airport (IATA: **LAX**) is one of the busiest airports in the world and Southern California's main gateway, handling more than 75 million passengers a year. It sits about 30 km (18 mi) southwest of Downtown LA, right on the Pacific coast near El Segundo and Playa del Rey.

## Overview

LAX is a sprawling nine-terminal airport built around a horseshoe-shaped central roadway that's become infamous for gridlock, especially during pickup and drop-off. The airport's biggest recent change is the **Automated People Mover (APM)**, a free train that opened in 2023 connecting every terminal to the new LAX/Metro Transit Center and a consolidated rental car facility — a long-overdue fix that's reshaping how people actually move through the airport.

## Terminals

LAX has nine passenger terminals (Terminals 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, and the Tom Bradley International Terminal, or TBIT), arranged around the central horseshoe. No single airline dominates — American, Delta, United, Southwest, and Alaska Airlines all operate significant terminal space, so always double-check your terminal before heading out, especially on a codeshare ticket.

## Airlines

LAX doesn't have one anchor hub carrier the way many airports do — American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, and United Airlines all treat it as a major West Coast gateway, alongside Southwest and Alaska Airlines for domestic routes. That multi-carrier structure makes LAX one of the most-connected airports in the world for both domestic and transpacific international travel.

## How to Get There

- **LAX People Mover + Metro:** The free Automated People Mover train connects all terminals to the LAX/Metro Transit Center, where you can transfer to Metro's C Line or K Line — a standard **$1.75** Metro fare gets you into the rail network, though it takes roughly 45–60 minutes to reach Downtown with a transfer.
- **FlyAway Bus:** **$9.75** one-way for a direct, no-transfer express bus to Union Station in Downtown LA, about 30–45 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Uber / Lyft:** Typically **$35–55** to Downtown or Santa Monica, about 30–50 minutes — note that rideshare pickup isn't at the terminal curb; you'll take a short shuttle bus to the designated pickup lot.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with [LAX FlyAway](https://www.flylax.com/flyaway-bus) or your ride-hailing app.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

Like other US airports, LAX has no departure passport control — only TSA security screening, for both domestic and international departures:

- **Domestic departures (TSA only):** Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **35–55 minutes** during busy morning and evening waves.
- **International departures (TSA only):** Similar TSA process to domestic, typically **30–40 minutes** off-peak, up to **50–75 minutes** at peak — TBIT handles a huge volume of long-haul departures, so plan for longer waits at typical evening international departure banks.
- **Arriving internationally?** Customs and Border Protection (CBP) processing happens on arrival at TBIT, separate from departure security — budget extra time if you're connecting onward after an international arrival.

## Parking

All nine terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay parking structures, plus more affordable off-site lots connected by shuttle. Given LAX's chronic road congestion, pre-booking parking online and building in extra time for the access roads is well worth it, especially during peak travel periods.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available across all terminals with no time limit.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure — LAX's road congestion alone can eat into your buffer even before you reach security.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for the concentrated evening departure bank of transpacific and transatlantic flights from TBIT.
- Because of the horseshoe road's notorious traffic, always add extra buffer for the drive or rideshare to the terminal itself — it's often the least predictable part of a LAX trip, more than security.

## Where to Eat

LAX has leaned into LA's food culture in recent years, with chef-driven concepts and local favorites (including an In-N-Out counter in Terminal 1) alongside the usual airport chains, spread across all nine terminals with the strongest selection in TBIT.

## Lounges

- **American Admirals Club** and **Delta Sky Club** locations are spread across their respective terminals, accessible with eligible tickets, elite status, or membership.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available, concentrated in TBIT.

## Duty-Free Shopping

LAX's duty-free selection is fairly standard for a major US gateway — fashion, cosmetics, and electronics — concentrated in TBIT's international departures area.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours, the new Automated People Mover makes reaching Metro rail into Downtown LA genuinely practical for the first time — something that wasn't realistic before 2023.
- If staying airside, TBIT has the best mix of lounges and LA-style dining for a long international wait.
- Given LAX's road congestion, if you're heading into the city on a tight layover, build in real buffer time for the return trip — traffic, not distance, is usually what eats the clock.
