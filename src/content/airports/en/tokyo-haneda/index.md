Tokyo Haneda (IATA: **HND**) is Japan's busiest airport by passenger volume and, thanks to its location just 15 km from central Tokyo, has become the preferred gateway for both domestic travel and a fast-growing share of international flights — a role that expanded significantly after slot reforms opened up more international capacity in the 2020s. It's the home base for both ANA and Japan Airlines.

## Overview

Haneda is split into three terminal buildings: Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 handle domestic flights (and a small amount of international), while **Terminal 3** is the dedicated international terminal. All three are linked by walkways, a free shuttle bus, and direct access from the Keikyu Line and Tokyo Monorail stations. Compared to Tokyo Narita, Haneda's big advantage is proximity — most transfers into the city take well under 30 minutes.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** Primarily Japan Airlines (JAL) and other Japan Airlines Group carriers, mostly domestic.
- **Terminal 2:** Primarily All Nippon Airways (ANA) and Star Alliance partners, mostly domestic, plus a growing number of international flights.
- **Terminal 3:** The dedicated international terminal, home to most foreign carriers and Haneda's international ANA/JAL flights, with an "Edo-koji" themed shopping and dining street styled after old Tokyo.

## Airlines

ANA and JAL dominate Haneda as their primary hub, covering an extensive domestic network across Japan plus growing long-haul international routes. International flag carriers — including major US, European, and Asian airlines — increasingly favor Haneda's international slots over Narita's, given the shorter transfer time into central Tokyo.

## How to Get There

- **Keikyu Line:** **¥310** to Shinagawa, about **13 minutes** — the cheapest and often fastest option, with direct through-service onto several Tokyo subway lines.
- **Tokyo Monorail:** **¥520** (or ¥519 with an IC card) to Hamamatsuchō, about 15–20 minutes, connecting directly onto the JR Yamanote Line loop.
- **Taxi:** Roughly **¥5,000–¥11,000** depending on your destination and time of day (higher late at night), about 30–40 minutes depending on traffic. Far pricier than the train but convenient with luggage or late at night.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check the operator's site for the current fare: [Keikyu](https://www.keikyu.co.jp/), [Tokyo Monorail](https://www.tokyo-monorail.co.jp/).*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only, no passport control. Typically **10–15 minutes** off-peak, up to **20–25 minutes** during the busiest morning and evening domestic banks.
- **International flights:** Security plus immigration. Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **40–50 minutes** at peak — Haneda is generally efficient and well-organized by international standards, with automated e-gates available for many nationalities on arrival.
- Terminal 3 (international) checkpoints are modern and well-staffed, so queues rarely reach the extremes seen at some other major hub airports.

## Parking

Multi-story car parks sit directly beneath or adjacent to all three terminals, with clear signage and Japanese-standard efficient operations. Rates are charged by time increment; pre-booking isn't as common in Japan as in Europe, but off-airport lots can still offer savings for longer stays.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available throughout all terminals with no time cap — one of the more traveler-friendly setups among major international hubs, and reliable enough for video calls during a layover.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** **60–90 minutes** before departure is typically enough, given Haneda's efficient domestic security process.
- **International flights:** **2.5–3 hours** ahead, especially for evening long-haul departures when Terminal 3 gets busiest.
- Immigration on arrival is generally fast thanks to e-gates, but budget extra time during Japanese holiday periods (New Year, Golden Week, Obon) when both domestic and international traffic peaks.

## Where to Eat

Haneda punches above its weight for airport dining — Terminal 3's Edo-koji street recreates an Edo-period Tokyo streetscape with ramen counters, sushi, and traditional sweets, while all three terminals have strong convenience-store and bento options for a quick, high-quality meal before boarding.

## Lounges

- **JAL Sakura Lounge** and **ANA Lounge** locations across Terminals 1, 2, and 3, accessible via business class tickets, elite status, or paid access for some lounges.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available, concentrated in Terminal 3.

## Duty-Free Shopping

Terminal 3's duty-free zone is extensive, with a strong focus on Japanese specialties — sake, whisky, regional snacks, and electronics — alongside the usual fashion and cosmetics brands. It's a genuinely good last stop for Japan-specific souvenirs, not just an afterthought.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 5+ hours and a visa situation that allows entry, central Tokyo is genuinely reachable — Shinagawa is 13 minutes away by Keikyu Line, making a short visit realistic even with a moderate layover.
- If staying airside, Terminal 3's Edo-koji street is worth exploring even without a city trip — it's an attraction in its own right.
- Haneda's compact, efficient layout makes overnight layovers manageable, with 24-hour convenience stores and ample seating, though dedicated free sleep pods are limited — check current airport announcements for capsule hotel-style options within the international terminal.
