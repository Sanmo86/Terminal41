Amsterdam Schiphol (IATA: **AMS**) is one of Europe's busiest hub airports and the home base of KLM and the SkyTeam alliance. Unlike most major hubs, Schiphol operates as a single unified terminal split into departure piers, which makes internal connections relatively straightforward compared to multi-terminal airports.

## Overview

Schiphol's "one terminal, multiple piers" design (piers B through M) means passport control and security are centralized, and once you're airside, moving between gates is mostly a matter of walking or taking an internal train between the E/F/EM piers. Royal Schiphol Group [reported 66.8 million passengers for 2024](https://www.businesstravelnewseurope.com/Air-Travel/Amsterdam-Schiphol-s-traffic-rose-by-8-in-2024), up 8% on 2023 but still below 2019's pre-pandemic 71.7 million.

## Terminals

There is technically one terminal building, divided into **Departure Halls 1, 2, and 3**, feeding into piers B, C, D, E, F/G, and M. Long-haul KLM/SkyTeam flights typically use pier E, F, or D. Schengen flights are concentrated in piers B, C, D, and G with separate (lighter) border checks.

## Airlines

KLM is the dominant carrier and anchor of the SkyTeam alliance at AMS, alongside partners like Delta, Air France, and Korean Air. Schiphol also hosts a large number of European and long-haul carriers, making it one of the best-connected airports for transatlantic and Europe-Asia routings.

## How to Get There

- **Train (NS):** From around **€6.20** for a single fare (prices vary with how you tap in/out via OV-pay or book ahead). Schiphol has its own railway station directly beneath the terminal, with 8 Sprinter trains an hour to Amsterdam Centraal in about **17 minutes**, plus frequent direct trains to other major Dutch cities.
- **Bus:** Regional and airport shuttle buses serve destinations not directly covered by rail; fares vary by route and operator, roughly 30–45 minutes depending on the stop.
- **Taxi/ride-hailing:** Available at designated ranks outside the arrivals halls. Metered at roughly €3.17/km plus €0.52/minute, which typically works out to **€40–€65** to central Amsterdam, about 20–30 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Bike rental:** Not available directly at the airport terminal, but easy to arrange once in the city if you're staying several days.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check the operator's site for the current fare: [NS](https://www.ns.nl/en).*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Schengen flights:** Security only. Typically **10–20 minutes** off-peak, up to **30 minutes** during busy periods.
- **Non-Schengen (international) flights:** Security plus passport control, mainly affecting piers E, F and D. Typically **20–30 minutes** off-peak, up to **45–60 minutes** during KLM's early-morning long-haul departure bank (06:00–08:00).
- Schiphol's centralized, single-terminal security setup generally moves faster than airports with per-terminal checkpoints — but the 06:00–08:00 wave is consistently the busiest window of the day.

## Parking

Schiphol offers both short-stay parking directly at the terminal (P1-P3) and cheaper long-stay lots (P20, P30) with shuttle transfer. Pre-booking online significantly reduces the cost compared to walk-up rates.

## Wifi

Schiphol provides free, unlimited wifi throughout the terminal with no time cap — one of the more traveler-friendly policies among major European hubs, making it a comfortable place to work through a layover.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Schengen flights:** **90 minutes** ahead is typically sufficient; allow closer to **2 hours** if departing during the 06:00–08:00 morning bank.
- **Non-Schengen/international flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for KLM long-haul departures from piers E and F, where security and passport control queues can build up during morning and evening banks.
- Early morning (06:00-08:00) is consistently the busiest window due to KLM's long-haul departure bank — if your itinerary lets you pick a flight time, a midday departure is usually far less stressful.

## Where to Eat

Schiphol has an unusually strong food scene for an airport, including sit-down Dutch and international restaurants, a well-known airport library and museum annex (Rijksmuseum outpost), and casual options throughout every pier. Bitterballen and stroopwafels are the classic Dutch airport snacks to try. Named options include **Grand Café Het Paleis** and **Schiphol Grand Café Plaza** for a sit-down meal, plus **Dutch Kitchen** for a more casual take on local dishes.

## Airport Hotel

**YOTELAIR Amsterdam Schiphol** sits airside, inside Lounge 2 at Terminal 2 — past security, so it's only usable if you're already through the departures process. Its compact, first-class-inspired cabins are built specifically for short layover stays rather than a full overnight, which makes it a genuinely different option from a landside airport hotel you'd need to clear security to leave again.

## Lounges

- **KLM Crown Lounges** (piers E, F/G, and M) are the primary lounges, accessible with business class tickets, SkyTeam Elite Plus status, or paid access for some lounges.
- Several independent and Priority Pass-affiliated lounges are also available across the terminal.

## Duty-Free Shopping

Schiphol is well known for its large duty-free zone, including Dutch specialties like cheese, tulip bulbs, and genever, alongside the usual fashion, electronics, and perfume outlets. The central shopping area between piers is deliberately designed as a walkable "airport city."

## Long Layover Tips

- With a layover of 5+ hours and eligibility to enter the Schengen area, a train into Amsterdam Centraal takes under 20 minutes, making a short city visit realistic.
- If staying airside, the Panorama Terrace (outside security, near Departure Hall 3) offers plane-spotting and fresh air — worth a visit if you have landside access.
- Schiphol's single-terminal layout means overnight layovers are relatively comfortable, with 24-hour food options and ample seating throughout the piers.
