New York's John F. Kennedy International Airport (IATA: **JFK**) is the busiest of the three major New York City airports and the main international gateway to the city, with a huge concentration of transatlantic and long-haul flights. It sits in Queens, about 26 km southeast of Manhattan, and is Delta's largest hub alongside a major JetBlue and international-carrier presence.

## Overview

JFK is laid out around a circular perimeter road (the old "Terminal City" loop) linking its terminals, with the AirTrain people-mover running the same loop plus a spur out to the subway and Long Island Rail Road. The airport is in the middle of a huge, multi-year redevelopment — several older terminals have already been replaced or are being replaced by the new **New Terminal One**, so signage and terminal numbers can shift; always double check your terminal on the day of travel.

## Terminals

JFK currently operates **five active terminals**: 1, 4, 5, 7, and 8 (former Terminals 2 and 3 have closed as part of the New Terminal One redevelopment, which will eventually consolidate several of these). Terminal 4 is the largest and hosts Delta's international flights and many SkyTeam and other foreign carriers; Terminal 5 is JetBlue's home; Terminal 8 (American Airlines/oneworld) and Terminal 7 round out the rest. The free AirTrain connects all terminals, parking, and the subway/LIRR station in about 5–10 minutes between any two points.

## Airlines

Delta is JFK's dominant carrier and uses it as a major international hub, alongside JetBlue (based in Terminal 5) and American Airlines (Terminal 8). JFK is also the primary New York gateway for the vast majority of foreign flag carriers — British Airways, Air France, Emirates, Cathay Pacific, and dozens more — making it one of the most internationally connected airports in North America.

## How to Get There

- **AirTrain + Subway:** **$11.75** total ($8.75 AirTrain + $3.00 subway) — the cheapest way into the city, via the A/E/J/Z lines from Jamaica or Howard Beach stations. About 60–80 minutes to Midtown Manhattan.
- **AirTrain + LIRR:** **$14–$16** total ($8.75 AirTrain + a Long Island Rail Road CityTicket fare) — the fastest public transit option, about 35–45 minutes from Jamaica Station to Penn Station.
- **Yellow Taxi (flat rate):** **$70** flat fare to anywhere in Manhattan (set by the NYC Taxi and Limousine Commission), plus tolls and tip — expect a total closer to $85–$95. A $5 rush-hour surcharge applies weekdays 4–8pm. About 45–60 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Ride-hailing:** Uber and Lyft operate from a dedicated pickup area; pricing is dynamic and can exceed the yellow taxi flat rate during surge periods.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check the operator's site for the current fare: [MTA](https://new.mta.info/), [NYC TLC](https://www.nyc.gov/site/tlc/index.page).*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

JFK's process is split differently than at EU airports — there's no outbound passport control in the US, only TSA security screening for all departing passengers:

- **Domestic departures (TSA security only):** Typically **20–35 minutes** off-peak, up to **45–60 minutes** during morning and early-evening banks. TSA PreCheck (where enrolled) cuts this dramatically.
- **International departures (TSA security only):** Similar TSA queues to domestic, typically **30–45 minutes** off-peak, up to **60–90 minutes** at peak — the wait comes from higher passenger volume at international-heavy terminals like T4, not from a separate passport check.
- **Arriving internationally?** Budget extra time for U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) on arrival — this is where the notoriously long JFK queues happen, especially when several wide-body flights land close together; **45–90+ minutes** is common at peak, longer during holidays. Global Entry can skip most of this line if you're enrolled.

## Parking

All five terminals have adjacent short-term parking garages, plus larger economy lots served by free AirTrain/shuttle connections. As with most major airports, pre-booking online is meaningfully cheaper than the terminal walk-up rate, especially for stays of several days.

## Wifi

Free wifi (via Boingo) is available across JFK, typically with a free introductory session before switching to paid tiers or slower speeds — coverage and exact limits vary by terminal. An eSIM is a solid backup if you need guaranteed connectivity.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure; push to 2.5 hours during the 06:00–09:00 and 16:00–19:00 peak banks.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead — TSA lines at internationally-heavy terminals (T4 especially) get busy fast in the evening long-haul departure wave.
- If you're arriving from an international flight with a tight connection, budget significant extra time for CBP — 90+ minutes isn't unusual during peak arrival banks.
- TSA PreCheck and Global Entry (via the Trusted Traveler Programs) are well worth enrolling in if you fly through JFK regularly.

## Where to Eat

JFK has built a reputation as one of the better US airports for food, particularly in Terminal 4's food hall, which features outposts of well-known NYC restaurants and chefs alongside the usual fast-food chains. Terminal 5 (JetBlue) also has a strong, JFK-Mood-Board-worthy food scene for a domestic terminal.

## Lounges

- **Delta Sky Club** locations in Terminal 4, available with eligible Delta tickets, elite status, or membership.
- **Admirals Club** (American Airlines) in Terminal 8.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are scattered across terminals, useful with the right travel credit card.

## Duty-Free Shopping

Terminal 4's departures area has the largest duty-free selection — fashion, electronics, and New York-themed souvenirs — with smaller selections in the other terminals geared toward last-minute essentials and gifts.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 6+ hours and a US visa/ESTA that allows re-entry, a trip into Manhattan is feasible via AirTrain+LIRR — but budget generously for CBP if you're arriving internationally, plus the return TSA line.
- If staying airside, Terminal 4 and Terminal 5 have the most comfortable seating and best food options for a long wait.
- There's no free airside sleep lounge — a Priority Pass day-pass or a paid rest area (available in some terminals) is worth it for overnight layovers.
