Dublin Airport (IATA: **DUB**) is Ireland's busiest airport and the primary base for both Aer Lingus and Ryanair, handling more than 32 million passengers a year. It sits about 10 km (6 mi) north of central Dublin.

## Overview

DUB operates two terminals and functions as Ireland's main international gateway, with an outsized role in global aviation that goes well beyond passenger numbers: Ireland is the world center of aircraft leasing, and the majority of the planet's leased commercial aircraft are managed by companies based around Dublin — a quiet but massive industry most travelers never notice.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** Primarily Ryanair and other budget carriers, plus some Aer Lingus flights.
- **Terminal 2:** Aer Lingus's main terminal and other full-service international carriers.

## Airlines

Aer Lingus, Ireland's flag carrier, operates its main base at DUB with an extensive European network plus growing transatlantic routes to North America. Ryanair, Europe's largest budget carrier, also uses Dublin as its largest base, operating an enormous short-haul network across Europe. Together they make DUB one of the best-connected airports in Europe for both full-service and budget travel.

## How to Get There

- **Dublin Bus / Aircoach:** **€7–10**, with multiple routes covering different parts of the city, about 30–40 minutes.
- **Taxi:** Typically **€30–40** to the city center, about 25–35 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Uber:** Generally comparable in price to a taxi, about 25–35 minutes — widely used in Dublin.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with [Dublin Bus](https://www.dublinbus.ie/) or your ride-hailing app.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **European flights:** Security only for most EU/UK routes. Typically **15–25 minutes** off-peak, up to **30–45 minutes** during Ryanair and Aer Lingus's busy morning and evening waves.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport/immigration control. Typically **25–40 minutes** off-peak, up to **45–65 minutes** during peak transatlantic departure banks.
- DUB handles an enormous volume of short-haul departures given Ryanair's base here — expect efficient but high-volume processing during peak periods.

## Parking

Both terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks, plus more affordable off-site options connected by shuttle. Pre-booking online is cheaper than the walk-up rate.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available across both terminals with no time limit.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **European flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure, especially during Ryanair's high-volume banks.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for Aer Lingus's transatlantic departures.
- Given Ryanair's huge base here, security can get genuinely busy during peak morning waves — don't underestimate a budget short-haul flight's security queue.

## Where to Eat

DUB has a good selection of Irish pub-style food alongside international chains, spread across both terminals.

## Lounges

- The **Aer Lingus Lounge** is available in Terminal 2 for eligible business class passengers, elite status holders, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

DUB's duty-free zone includes genuine Irish specialties — whiskey and wool goods — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 4+ hours and a visa situation permitting entry, central Dublin's Temple Bar and Trinity College are reachable by bus in about 30–40 minutes.
- If staying airside, Terminal 2 has the best mix of lounges and dining given Aer Lingus's presence.
- Given how much of the world's aircraft-leasing business is quietly headquartered around Dublin, it's worth appreciating that this relatively modest-sized airport plays an outsized role in how the entire global airline industry gets its planes.
