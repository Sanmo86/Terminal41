Cancún International Airport (IATA: **CUN**) is one of the busiest airports in Latin America and a key focus city for both Viva Aerobus and Volaris, with more than 30 million passengers a year. It sits about 20 km south of the Hotel Zone, on Mexico's Caribbean coast.

## Overview

CUN operates three active terminals and serves a city with a genuinely unusual origin story: Cancún was deliberately planned and built from scratch starting in the early 1970s, when Mexican government planners identified an almost uninhabited stretch of coastline and sandbar as ideal for a purpose-built resort. What's now one of the busiest airports in Latin America didn't exist in any meaningful form barely five decades ago — a striking example of a destination engineered into being within a single generation.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 2:** Handles mostly domestic Mexican carriers.
- **Terminal 3:** The largest and busiest, handling the bulk of international traffic.
- **Terminal 4:** Handles additional international and charter traffic during peak tourist season.

## Airlines

Viva Aerobus and Volaris, two of Mexico's major low-cost carriers, both treat CUN as a key focus city, running extensive domestic networks alongside international routes to the United States, Canada, and beyond. Given Cancún's status as a top global beach destination, the airport also draws a wide range of international carriers serving it directly, especially during North American winter and spring break seasons.

## Getting There

- **Colectivo (shared van):** Around **200–300 MXN**, a shared shuttle dropping at multiple hotels along the way, about 30–45 minutes.
- **ADO bus:** Around **90–150 MXN**, direct to downtown Cancún or the Hotel Zone, about 30–40 minutes.
- **Official airport taxi:** Around **600–900 MXN** from the fixed-rate booth inside the terminal, about 20–25 minutes — notably pricier than ride-hailing options outside the airport perimeter.

*Prices above are approximate and subject to change — always check current fares at the ADO or colectivo counters inside the terminal.*

## Security and Passport Control Wait Times

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **15–20 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **25–35 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **25–35 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **40–60 minutes** during peak tourist-season departure banks.
- CUN sees strong seasonal variation tied to North American winter and spring break travel — expect longer waits during those windows.

## Parking

All three terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay parking, with clear signage.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available in the terminal with a limited free session.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** before departure, especially during peak winter and spring break tourist season.
- Given CUN's heavy seasonal tourist traffic, building in extra buffer time during North American school holidays is worth the caution.

## Where to Eat

CUN has a good selection of Yucatecan and broader Mexican cuisine alongside international chains, spread across its terminals.

## Lounges

- The **American Express Centurion Lounge** is available for eligible cardholders.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

CUN's duty-free area includes Mexican tequila and Cuban cigars — notably more accessible here than in many other countries — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Layover Tips

- With 4 hours or more and a visa situation that allows entry, the Hotel Zone's beaches are reachable via ADO bus or colectivo in about 30–40 minutes — though most visitors treat Cancún as a destination rather than a connecting point.
- If staying airside, CUN's duty-free tequila and cigar selection is genuinely notable compared to most international airports.
- Given Cancún's origin as a purpose-built resort, nearly everything about the destination — including the airport itself — reflects deliberate large-scale tourism planning rather than organic growth around an older city.
