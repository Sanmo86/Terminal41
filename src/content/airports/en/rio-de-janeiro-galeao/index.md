Rio de Janeiro's Galeão International Airport, officially Tom Jobim International Airport (IATA: **GIG**), is Rio's main international gateway, sitting on Governador Island about 20 km north of the city center. It's the primary hub for international flights into Rio, while a smaller downtown airport (Santos Dumont) handles the São Paulo air shuttle and some regional routes.

## Overview

Galeão operates two terminals connected by a walkway and shuttle bus. The airport has undergone significant modernization since a major expansion tied to the 2014 World Cup and 2016 Olympics, and today has capacity for well over double its current traffic — meaning it's generally less crowded and easier to navigate than many international hubs of comparable size.

## Terminals

- **Terminal 1:** Primarily domestic flights, with 26 gates.
- **Terminal 2:** International flights and some domestic routes, with 27 gates. Most long-haul arrivals and departures use this terminal.

Always confirm your terminal when booking transport, since both handle a mix of traffic.

## Airlines

LATAM Brasil, GOL, and Azul are the dominant domestic carriers connecting Rio to the rest of Brazil, while Galeão is also the main international gateway for flights to North America, Europe, and other South American countries via major international airlines.

## How to Get There

- **BRT / Municipal Bus:** **R$5** flat fare — the cheapest option, connecting to the wider BRT network with several stops along the way, about 40–60 minutes depending on traffic and destination.
- **Official Taxi:** **R$50–R$90** to central Rio, metered on the official city tariff (higher rate 21:00–06:00, Sundays, and holidays), about 30–45 minutes depending on traffic. Use the official taxi ranks inside the terminal.
- **Uber / 99:** Typically **30–40% cheaper** than an official taxi, with GPS tracking and cashless payment — a popular choice for visitors, about 30–45 minutes.
- **Air-conditioned coach (Real Auto Ônibus):** Runs to the city center (Novo Rio bus station) and along the beach neighborhoods (Copacabana, Ipanema, Leblon) via the coastal avenue — a comfortable middle-ground option between the municipal bus and a taxi.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current rates at the official taxi counter or your transport app.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **15–25 minutes** off-peak, up to **30–40 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **25–40 minutes** off-peak, up to **50–70 minutes** during peak international departure banks, most common in the evening.
- Thanks to the airport's post-Olympics capacity expansion, Galeão generally has more breathing room than many similarly-sized international airports — long queues are the exception rather than the rule outside peak holiday travel periods.

## Parking

Both terminals have adjacent short- and long-stay car parks. As a less congested airport relative to its size, parking is generally straightforward; confirm current rates directly with RIOgaleão since pricing has evolved with the airport's ongoing modernization.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available in both terminals with a time-limited free session, typical for the region. An eSIM with a Brazilian data plan is a reliable backup, particularly useful for ride-hailing apps and maps on arrival.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is generally sufficient.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for evening departures when international traffic peaks.
- During Rio's peak tourist season (Carnival, New Year's, summer months of December–March), build in extra buffer time for both traffic to the airport and airport processing.

## Where to Eat

Both terminals have a mix of Brazilian classics — pão de queijo, açaí, coxinha — alongside coffee bars and quick-service international chains post-security. Options are more limited than at Latin America's largest hubs but cover the essentials well.

## Lounges

Lounge options include **LATAM** and **GOL**-affiliated lounges for eligible passengers, plus several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges, mainly concentrated in Terminal 2's international departures area.

## Duty-Free Shopping

The duty-free area leans into Brazilian specialties — Brazilian gemstones and jewelry, cachaça, and locally roasted coffee — alongside the usual fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands, making it a solid last stop for gifts.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 4+ hours and a visa situation that allows entry, Rio's beach neighborhoods (Copacabana, Ipanema) are reachable via the airport coach or a taxi — but factor in Rio's often heavy traffic when planning your return.
- If staying airside, Terminal 2's international departures area has the best mix of seating and food options for a longer wait.
- Lounge access is worth prioritizing here if you have it — seating and rest options in the general terminal areas are more limited than at some larger hubs.
