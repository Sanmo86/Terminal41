Murtala Muhammed International Airport (IATA: **LOS**) is Nigeria's busiest airport and the main international gateway to Lagos, Africa's largest city by population, serving more than 8 million passengers a year. It sits about 22 km (14 mi) from Lagos Island, the historic commercial heart of the city.

## Overview

LOS operates two main terminal areas — the domestic MM2 terminal and the international MMIA terminal — serving as the primary entry point to an enormous and fast-growing megacity. As Nigeria's main gateway, the airport connects West Africa's largest economy to an extensive domestic network plus international routes across Africa, Europe, and beyond.

## Terminals

- **MM2 (Domestic):** Handles domestic flights across Nigeria.
- **MMIA (International):** Handles all international arrivals and departures, including Air Peace's growing long-haul network.

## Airlines

Air Peace, Nigeria's largest airline by fleet size, operates its main base at LOS with an extensive domestic network across the country plus a growing international portfolio including long-haul routes. As Nigeria's primary gateway, LOS also handles significant traffic from major international carriers connecting Lagos to the rest of the world.

## How to Get There

- **Taxi:** **₦5,000–10,000** via the official taxi counter inside the terminal, about 40–60 minutes depending on Lagos's notoriously heavy traffic.
- **Uber / Bolt:** Generally comparable in price to a taxi, about 40–60 minutes — both apps are widely used in Lagos.
- **Danfo bus:** The cheapest option by far, but not recommended for travelers with luggage given crowding and informal routes.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with your ride-hailing app or the airport's official taxi counter.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **15–25 minutes** off-peak, up to **30–45 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **25–40 minutes** off-peak, up to **45–65 minutes** during peak international departure banks.
- As Nigeria's main international gateway, LOS handles a significant volume of passengers — build in extra buffer time given the airport's overall traffic levels.

## Parking

LOS has parking available at both terminal areas, with clear signage for short- and long-stay options.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available in the terminal with a time-limited free session.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is typically enough.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially given Lagos's traffic and the airport's overall passenger volume.
- Lagos traffic is notoriously heavy and unpredictable — always build substantial extra buffer time into your journey to the airport, regardless of time of day.

## Where to Eat

LOS has a solid selection of Nigerian and West African cuisine alongside more standard airport dining options.

## Lounges

- The **Air Peace Lounge** is available for eligible business class passengers, elite status holders, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

LOS's duty-free zone includes Nigerian textiles and local crafts alongside a more limited selection of standard international brands compared to larger global hubs.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 5+ hours and a visa situation permitting entry, central Lagos and Lagos Island are reachable by taxi, though always budget significant time given the city's traffic.
- If staying airside, the Air Peace Lounge offers a comfortable option for a longer wait.
- Given Lagos's traffic is genuinely unpredictable at almost any hour, never cut it close on the return trip to the airport — leave far more buffer time than you'd expect to need.
