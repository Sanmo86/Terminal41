Keflavík International Airport (IATA: **KEF**) is Iceland's main international gateway and the hub of Icelandair, with more than 9 million passengers a year. It sits about 50 km southwest of central Reykjavík, on the volcanically active Reykjanes peninsula.

## Overview

KEF operates a single terminal — Flugstöð Leifs Eiríkssonar, named after the Norse explorer — and sits on a genuinely striking landscape: a lava field on the Reykjanes peninsula, one of the most volcanically active regions in the world. Several eruptions have occurred within a few kilometers of the airport in recent years, and the surrounding terrain of black basalt rock makes the approach unlike almost any other major airport. As Icelandair's hub, KEF also anchors a well-known "stopover" model connecting North America and Europe via Iceland.

## Terminals

KEF operates a single integrated terminal handling all Schengen and international traffic, including the full Icelandair and Play network.

## Airlines

Icelandair, Iceland's flag carrier, operates its hub at KEF, running an extensive North America–Europe network built around Iceland as a stopover destination — a model that has let the airline connect dozens of cities on both continents through a single hub. Play, a newer Icelandic low-cost carrier, also bases significant operations at KEF.

## Getting There

- **Flybus:** **3,999 ISK**, a direct service to central Reykjavík timed to flight arrivals, about 45 minutes.
- **Airport Direct / Reykjavík Excursions:** Around **4,000 ISK**, with an optional hotel drop-off, about 45 minutes.
- **Taxi:** Metered, typically **15,000–20,000 ISK** given the distance — notably expensive by international standards, about 40–50 minutes.

*Prices above are approximate and subject to change — always check current fares with [Flybus](https://www.flybus.is/) or [Reykjavík Excursions](https://www.re.is/).*

## Security and Passport Control Wait Times

- **Schengen flights:** Security only for most intra-European routes. Typically **15–20 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **25–35 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **20–30 minutes** during off-peak hours, up to **35–55 minutes** during peak departure banks, notably Icelandair's transatlantic connecting waves.
- KEF's small single-terminal layout generally keeps processing efficient outside of peak connection banks.

## Parking

The terminal has adjacent short- and long-stay parking, with clear signage. Booking online ahead of arrival is cheaper than the walk-up rate.

## Wifi

Free, unlimited wifi is available throughout the airport with no time limit.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Schengen flights:** Arrive **2 hours** before departure.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** before departure, especially during Icelandair's peak transatlantic connecting banks.
- Given KEF's role as a stopover hub, many passengers are connecting rather than starting or ending their trip there — factor in extra time if your connection falls during a busy bank.

## Where to Eat

KEF has a solid selection of Icelandic seafood and lamb dishes alongside international chains, spread across the terminal.

## Lounges

- The **Icelandair Saga Lounge** is available for eligible business class passengers, elite status holders, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

KEF's duty-free area includes genuinely Icelandic specialties — wool goods like Icelandic sweaters (lopapeysur) and Brennivín schnapps — alongside the usual international fashion, cosmetics, and electronics brands.

## Layover Tips

- With 5 hours or more and a visa situation that allows entry, central Reykjavík is reachable via Flybus in about 45 minutes — many travelers use Iceland's famous "stopover" program to turn a layover into a short standalone trip.
- If staying airside, KEF's basalt-lava surroundings are visible from the terminal windows — a striking reminder of Iceland's geology even without leaving the building.
- Given the distance to Reykjavík and Iceland's unpredictable weather, build in extra buffer time for winter layovers especially.
