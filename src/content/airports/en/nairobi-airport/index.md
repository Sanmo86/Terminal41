Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (IATA: **NBO**) is Kenya's busiest airport and Kenya Airways' principal hub, serving more than 9 million passengers a year as East Africa's most important aviation connecting point. It sits about 15 km (9 mi) southeast of central Nairobi — directly adjacent to Nairobi National Park.

## Overview

NBO's most distinctive feature is its neighbor: Nairobi National Park, a genuine wildlife reserve home to lions, giraffes, rhinos, and other free-roaming African wildlife, sits immediately next to the airport. It's one of the only major international airports anywhere in the world where you can genuinely see wild animals near the runway on approach or departure. As Kenya Airways' hub, NBO anchors an extensive network across East and Southern Africa.

## Terminals

NBO operates a single main terminal complex, rebuilt and expanded following a 2013 fire, with ongoing expansion projects to increase capacity for both domestic and international flights.

## Airlines

Kenya Airways, Kenya's flag carrier, operates its principal hub at NBO with an extensive network across East, Southern, and West Africa plus international routes to Europe, Asia, and beyond as a SkyTeam member. That hub concentration makes NBO the most important connecting point for travel within East Africa specifically, and a key gateway for safari tourism to Kenya and neighboring countries.

## How to Get There

- **Taxi:** **KES 1,500–2,500** via the official taxi counter inside the terminal, about 25–40 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Uber / Bolt:** Generally comparable in price to a taxi, about 25–40 minutes — both apps are widely used in Nairobi.
- **SGR / bus:** Cheaper public transport options exist but are less direct, about 30–45 minutes.

*Prices above are indicative and can change — always check current fares with your ride-hailing app or the airport's official taxi counter.*

## Getting Through Security and Passport Control

- **Domestic flights:** Security only. Typically **15–20 minutes** off-peak, up to **25–35 minutes** during busy periods.
- **International flights:** Security plus passport control. Typically **25–35 minutes** off-peak, up to **40–60 minutes** during Kenya Airways' peak connecting banks.
- As East Africa's primary connecting hub, NBO sees significant transfer traffic — follow transfer signage carefully if connecting internationally.

## Parking

NBO has parking available at the terminal, with clear signage for short- and long-stay options.

## Wifi

Free wifi is available in the terminal for travelers.

## Best Times to Arrive

- **Domestic flights:** **90 minutes** before departure is typically enough.
- **International flights:** Arrive **3 hours** ahead, especially for Kenya Airways' peak connecting banks.
- Given NBO's role as East Africa's main hub, allow generous time if connecting between two Kenya Airways flights during peak banks.

## Where to Eat

NBO has a solid selection of Kenyan and East African cuisine alongside more standard airport dining options.

## Lounges

- The **Kenya Airways Pride Center** lounge is available for eligible business class passengers, elite status holders, or paid access.
- Several **Priority Pass**-affiliated lounges are also available.

## Duty-Free Shopping

NBO's duty-free zone includes genuine Kenyan specialties — coffee, tea, and local crafts — reflecting the country's major agricultural exports.

## Long Layover Tips

- With 5+ hours and a visa situation permitting entry (Kenya's e-visa system makes this straightforward for most nationalities), a quick visit to Nairobi National Park itself is a genuinely unique way to spend a layover, given its proximity to the airport.
- If staying airside, the Pride Center lounge has a solid mix of comfort and dining given Kenya Airways' hub presence.
- Given NBO's role as East Africa's connecting hub, double-check your connecting gate as soon as you land if transferring between Kenya Airways flights.
